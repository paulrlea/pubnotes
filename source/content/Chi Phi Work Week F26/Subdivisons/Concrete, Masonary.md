### Basement pit o' Doom
Materials Needed
- Crushed gravel (¾-inch minus or dense-grade aggregate)
- Premixed concrete bags
- Water and a mixing tub or portable mixer
- Wheelbarrow and shovel
- Hand tamper or plate compactor
- Sturdy wooden board (screed board)
- Mason's trowel 
Instructions
- Clean the Hole: Remove all loose dirt, old broken concrete, and debris from the cavity.
- Dry the Area: Clear out any standing water so the subgrade is stable.
- Add Gravel Base: Pour 4 to 6 inches of crushed gravel into the bottom of the hole.
- Compact the Gravel: Pack the gravel down tight with a hand tamper to stop future shifting.
- Mix the Concrete: Stir your concrete mix with clean water in a tub until it looks like thick oatmeal.
- Pour the Concrete: Dump the wet concrete over the packed gravel bed.
- Spread and Level: Push the concrete into all corners and smooth the top flat with your shovel.
- Screed the Surface: Slide a wooden board back and forth across the top to match the height of the old floor.
- Smooth and Finish: Use a trowel to make the top smooth and clean.
- Cure the Concrete: Keep the surface damp for a few days so it gets as hard as possible. 
### Front Pillar Repair
What You Need
- Hammer and cold chisel
- Wire brush and shop vacuum
- Concrete bonding adhesive
- Quick-setting cement or vinyl concrete patch mix (such as [Quikrete Quick-Setting Cement](https://www.google.com/search?ibp=oshop&prds=pvt:hg,pvo:48,mid:576462732879114185,imageDocid:1702534745694836057,gpcid:4900696282836066062,headlineOfferDocid:7796583450931666839,catalogid:17664197820228499696,productDocid:9942353138611780196,rds:PC_4900696282836066062%7CPROD_PC_4900696282836066062&q=product&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwi56fS_jbKWAxWIGVkFHT9xFp0Qxa4PegoIAAgACAAIDhAK))
- Scrap plywood and weights/cinder blocks for a form
- Trowel, margin trowel, and edging tool
- Spray bottle or damp sponge 

Step 1: Prep and Clean the Area
- Chip away all loose, soft, or crumbling concrete using your hammer and chisel.
- Undercut the edges of the hole so the inner part is slightly wider than the surface opening, which locks the patch in place.
- Scrub away all dust and loose particles with a wire brush and vacuum the area completely. 
Step 2: Set Up and Prime
- Drill anchor holes and insert masonry screws if you are repairing a large broken corner or deep edge.
- Saturate the repair zone with water so the old dry concrete does not suck moisture out of your new mix, but wipe away standing puddles (Saturated Surface Dry condition).
- Paint a liberal layer of concrete bonding adhesive over the exposed old concrete.
Step 3: Build a Form and Apply the Mix
- Clamp or brace a piece of oiled scrap wood against the step face to act as a mold for missing edges or corners.
- Mix your quick-setting cement with water until it has a heavy, moldable consistency.
- Press the material firmly into all corners and crevices with a trowel to eliminate air pockets.
- Screed the surface flat and use an edging tool to recreate rounded step profiles. 
Step 4: Cure and Finish
- Smooth the final surface gently using a moist sponge. 
- Carefully remove the wood form after the material sets (usually 20 to 30 minutes for quick-setting mixes), and smooth any rough transition marks.
- Keep the patch damp by misting it with water or covering it with plastic sheeting for at least 48 hours.
